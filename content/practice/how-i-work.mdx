I design and I build, and I do not hand one to the other. The interesting part is not either skill. It is the loop between them, and what I write down so the loop gets faster each time it turns.

## The loop

<Steps title="INTENT, BUILD, VERIFY, LEARN">

1. Sketch

   Sit together, talk it through, draw it on paper. Nothing is built before the idea has a shape.

2. Build the real thing

   In code, with real data and the real components, as early as possible. A picture of an interface hides the hard parts.

3. **Verify what renders**

   It is not done because it compiles. It is done when the page in the browser does what it should, at every size, for keyboard and screen reader too.

4. Encode the lesson

   A bug becomes a check. A decision becomes a spec. A taste call becomes a rule with its reason written beside it.

5. Reuse

   The next change starts from the specs, tokens, rules and skills, not from memory.

</Steps>

## Reports that fix themselves

At Spera a bug report captures the route, the element under the pointer, its component, screenshots, the steps and the console. Triage turns it into a prompt that already carries the matching specs and checks. The reporting was designed to make the fixing cheaper.

<GraphTimeline title="ONE REPORT">

- Capture: the real surface, not a sentence in chat
- Triage: owner, priority, evidence
- Route: the smallest spec and the right checks
- **Verify: on the rendered product**
- Close: and write down why it broke

</GraphTimeline>

## On this site

This site is built the same way, mostly with Claude Code as the hands and me as the art director. The [loader eye](/making/loader-eye) is the clearest example.

- **A lab before the page.** Each part is built on a bench page where it can be shown alone, in any era, before it goes near the site.
- **One part at a time, signed off.** Nothing moves on until I have looked at real renders and said yes. Eras go in batches of three.
- **Ideas compete.** For a hard call, several concepts are written and judged before one is built.
- **Dead ends stay on record.** Rejected rounds are kept, because they explain the one that shipped. See [the openings that lost](/making/opening-rounds).
- **The rules are written down.** Thirteen skills hold what was learned about the eye, each one job with its reasons. They are on the [Skills shelf](/notebook#skills).

## What I will not do

- Ship a picture of an interface instead of the interface.
- Spend a signal on the wrong thing: one accent, one primary action, one thing that moves.
- Call it done before it is seen working.
- Keep a lesson in my head when it could be a check.
