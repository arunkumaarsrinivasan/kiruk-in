## The rule

At rest these move, and nothing else:

- the eye itself;
- its one sign in the pupil;
- grain, stepped at 12 Hz;
- the era's own idle state, small and slow: a caret that blinks at most twice a second, a clock digit.

Nothing scales or breathes the frame. There are never more than two and a half large changes of brightness in a second.

## Why

Motion is the cheapest way to make a render feel alive, and the fastest way to make it tiring. A real eye is mostly still. It jumps, rests, and jumps again.

But an era with no idle life at all reads as a photograph. So each era gets one local thing that keeps time, and it is kept small.

## On phones and for reduced motion

The eye goes calmer still. Jumps are fewer, era changes are rate-limited, and nothing asks for a permission.
